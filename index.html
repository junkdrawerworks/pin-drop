    How to play

    • Drag the ball sideways to choose where you stand.
    • Swipe up from the ball to roll. Which way you swipe is where it goes, and how fast you swipe is how hard it’s thrown.
    • On a trackpad, press and hold with one finger and flick up with another. With a mouse, hold the button down, flick up and let go.
    • On a keyboard, Left and Right move the ball and H changes the hook (none, left, right). Hold Space to wind up the throw and let go to roll.
    • Curve your swipe to hook. Let the end of it curl to the left and the ball hooks left on the dry end of the lane, the last twenty feet.
    • Aim for the pocket, just right of the head pin (just left of it if you hook to the right). Straight balls work too; a ball that hooks in strikes more often.
    • Bumpers keep the ball out of the gutters, for kids and first-timers: switch them on next to a player’s name before you bowl. A game with bumpers doesn’t count toward a best.
    • A head start evens up a game between a first-timer and a regular: tap a player’s ball before you bowl for 10 to 50 pins, added to their total at the end. It doesn’t count toward a best either.
    • Playing alone? Add a computer player on the title, at Rookie, Regular or Pro. It bowls its own turns, and a tap on the lane hurries it along. Its games never count toward a best.
    • Scoring is the real thing: a strike scores 10 plus your next two balls, a spare 10 plus your next one, and a strike or spare in the tenth earns the extra balls to count it, up to three.
    • A ring round a count on the scorecard marks a split: a first ball that left pins standing with a gap between them.
